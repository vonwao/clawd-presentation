# Alt Outline A — "Start With the Human"

**Arc:** Personal → Philosophical → Technical → Practical
**Thesis:** Begin with the felt experience of change, then zoom out to understand it.
**Tone:** Intimate opening, expanding outward.

---

## Slide 1 — Title

**Clawd: When Intelligence Grew Hands**
Otto von Wachter

---

## Slide 2 — Personal story (open cold with it)

**"I stopped writing last Tuesday."**

Not because I couldn't. Because something else wrote it — and I just... approved it.

Speaker note: No framing, no context. Drop the audience straight into the uncanny feeling. The story IS the hook.

---

## Slide 3 — The question that changed

Before: "Can AI do this?"
Now: "Should I still do this myself?"

Speaker note: Let this hang. The shift isn't capability — it's identity.

---

## Slide 4 — What it reveals about us

- What is judgment vs. routine?
- What work is worth doing ourselves?
- What should never be delegated?
- What does "I did this" mean now?

Speaker note: The philosophical punch comes EARLY in this version. Audience hasn't been told how agents work yet — they're sitting with the human disorientation first.

---

## Slide 5 — So what actually happened? (the shift)

From answers → to actions
From language → to agency
From "tell me" → to "handle it"

Speaker note: NOW explain. The audience is curious because they felt the strangeness first.

---

## Slide 6 — What "hands" mean

Hands = tools + loops:
- write / edit
- browse / research
- run code and scripts
- call APIs
- try → observe → adjust → repeat

Speaker note: Ground it. Tangible list relaxes the room after the existential opening.

---

## Slide 7 — The agent loop (diagram)

Goal → Context → Plan → Act → Observe → Evaluate → (repeat)

Speaker note: "This is the architecture of doing. Not one prompt — a loop of intention."

---

## Slide 8 — Mini-case: Morning inbox triage

**Before (~45 min):**
- Open inbox, scan 40+ emails
- Decide importance manually
- Draft replies one by one
- Context-switch between threads

**With agent (~10 min):**
- Agent pre-sorts by urgency and topic
- Drafts replies for routine threads
- Flags 3-4 items requiring human judgment
- Human reviews, edits tone, sends

**Delta:** Human effort shifts from sorting to deciding.

Speaker note: Different example than competitor brief — this one is universal. Everyone has an inbox.

---

## Slide 9 — The uncanny middle

- Sounds like a person, isn't one
- Acts like an employee, has no stake
- Makes errors that feel human but aren't — confident, consistent, alien

Speaker note: Name the tension honestly. We haven't built social norms for this yet.

---

## Slide 10 — Why AGI debates feel stuck

People mix up five different variables:
- Intelligence (reasoning)
- Capability (skills)
- Agency (acting)
- Autonomy (acting unasked)
- Reliability (acting correctly)

Speaker note: "One word hides five questions. That's why nobody agrees."

---

## Slide 11 — The real threshold: trust

"Is it smart?" → not enough
"Can I trust it?" → the real question

Trust = scope + visibility + reversibility

Speaker note: This is where it becomes practical and real.

---

## Slide 12 — What to do with this

Three questions to take home:
1. Where do I want agency — where don't I?
2. What do I require before I trust it?
3. What becomes more valuable in a world of delegation?

---

## Slide 13 — Closing

"The future is not AI replacing humans.
It's humans learning to direct intelligence that acts.
The hands are here. The question is what we build with them."

---

**Why this ordering might work better:**
- Opens with vulnerability and recognition, not abstraction
- Audience feels the shift before being told how it works
- Technical explanation arrives as relief ("oh, that's how it works")
- Shorter — 13 slides instead of 15, tighter arc
- The philosophical questions land harder when they come before the mechanism
