## Slide 3 — The shift in one sentence

**On slide:**

* **From answers → to actions**
* **From language → to agency**
* **From ‘tell me’ → to ‘handle it’**

**Speaker note:** This is the core: not just smarter text—goal-driven behavior in the world.

---

## Slide 4 — What do I mean by “hands”?

**On slide:**

* Hands = access to tools:

  * write / edit
  * click / browse
  * run code
  * call APIs
  * schedule / message
* And: **loops** (try → observe → adjust)

**Speaker note:** Keep it concrete. People relax when it’s tangible.

---

## Slide 5 — The agent loop (simple diagram slide)

**On slide:**

* **Goal → Plan → Act → Observe → Improve → (repeat)**

**Speaker note:** “This loop is the heartbeat. It’s why this feels different from ‘prompting.’”

---

## Slide 6 — A 30-second story (everyday example)

**Title:** “A normal task, now delegated”
**On slide:**

* “Plan a weekend trip”
* “Summarize options”
* “Draft itinerary”
* “Book / reserve (with approval)”

**Speaker note:** Stress: the magic is not the itinerary; it’s the *workflow completion*.

---

## Slide 7 — The texture of the near future

**On slide (short, punchy):**

* More things happen “in the background”
* Waiting turns into reviewing
* Work becomes supervising
* Effort becomes less visible

**Speaker note:** “Life feels faster—but also stranger: outcomes appear without the familiar grind.”

---

## Slide 8 — What is AGI (and why people fight about it)?

**On slide:**

* People mix different questions:

  * **Intelligence** (reasoning)
  * **Capability** (skills)
  * **Agency** (acting)
  * **Autonomy** (acting unasked)
  * **Reliability** (acting correctly)

**Speaker note:** “AGI debates often fail because we argue about one word that hides five variables.”

---

## Slide 9 — A clean definition that doesn’t start wars

**Title:** “A useful working definition”
**On slide:**

* **AGI = broad competence across domains + adaptability**
* But society may be transformed by:

  * **general agency** + **scale** + **integration**
  * even without consensus “AGI”

**Speaker note:** Key line: “We might not agree on AGI, but we’ll feel the consequences anyway.”

---

## Slide 10 — The real threshold: delegation

**On slide:**

* The question shifts from:

  * “Is it smart?” → to → **“Can I trust it?”**
* Trust =

  * scope (what it’s allowed to do)
  * visibility (what it did)
  * reversibility (can we undo it)

**Speaker note:** This is where the talk becomes real for the audience.

---

## Slide 11 — The danger of ‘hands’ (without fear-mongering)

**On slide:**

* Biggest risks aren’t sci-fi:

  * confident wrong actions
  * permission creep
  * quiet failures
  * accountability blur (“who did this?”)

**Speaker note:** Calm tone: “This is like giving a new employee admin access on day one.”

---

## Slide 12 — The human shift (what changes in us)

**On slide:**

* Humans move toward:

  * **judgment**
  * **taste**
  * **values**
  * **responsibility**
* Less time on:

  * rote execution
  * coordination overhead

**Speaker note:** “The bottleneck becomes meaning and choice—not effort.”

---

## Slide 13 — The New Mirror (the philosophical punch)

**On slide:**

* When we build agents, we’re forced to ask:

  * What is good work?
  * What deserves human attention?
  * What should never be delegated?
  * What does responsibility mean now?

**Speaker note:** This is your “TED moment.” Slow down.

---

## Slide 14 — What to do with this (gentle call to action)

**On slide:**

* 3 questions to take home:

  1. **Where do I want agency—where don’t I?**
  2. **What do I require for trust?**
  3. **What becomes more valuable in a world of delegation?**

**Speaker note:** Keep it universal: life, work, relationships, creativity.

---

## Slide 15 — Closing line (minimal)

**On slide:**

* **“We may argue about AGI for years.
  But we’re already living through the rise of agency.”** 🦞

**Speaker note:** End with wonder + responsibility, not hype.
