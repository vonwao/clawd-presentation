# Presentation Improvement Brainstorm

## Purpose
Generate high-impact next iterations for the talk, with extra depth on:
- `1)` Personal story slide
- `2)` Concrete mini-case (before/after workflow)

Target: make the deck more memorable, trusted, and discussion-ready without breaking the 12-15 minute runtime.

## Success Criteria
- Audience remembers at least one concrete example 24 hours later.
- Talk feels grounded in real usage, not abstract forecasting.
- Q&A can go deeper without derailing the main arc.

## 1) Personal Story Slide (Highest Priority)
### Why
- Adds credibility and emotional anchor.
- Converts “interesting thesis” into “I have seen this happen.”

### Placement Options
- Preferred: after Slide 5 (`Agency changes your Tuesday`).
- Alternate: after Slide 2 as an early hook.

### Story Framework (60-90 sec)
- `Context`: where you were / what problem existed.
- `Moment`: what the agent did that surprised you.
- `Shift`: what changed in your behavior after that.
- `Takeaway`: one sentence that ties back to agency.

### Candidate Story Prompts
- “The first time I delegated a real task to an agent and stopped micromanaging.”
- “A week where I realized I became the reviewer, not the producer.”
- “A workflow that used to take a morning, now done before coffee.”

### Slide Format Ideas
- Option A: single large quote + one supporting line.
- Option B: 3-step timeline (`Before -> Moment -> After`).
- Option C: “I used to / now I” split panel.

### Draft Copy Starters
- “The first task I trusted an agent with was small. The implication was not.”
- “I noticed I had stopped asking ‘can it do this?’ and started asking ‘should I still do this myself?’”

### Risks
- Too anecdotal: solve by ending with a generalizable lesson.
- Too long: cap at 90 sec, no side stories.

### Open Inputs Needed
- Which real story from your experience has the strongest emotional turn?
- Which domain is most authentic: coding, operations, writing, or scheduling?

## 2) Concrete Mini-Case (Highest Priority)
### Why
- Converts conceptual claims into visible mechanism.
- Increases audience trust quickly.

### Placement Options
- Preferred: after Slide 6 (`Agent Loop`) as proof of loop behavior.
- Alternate: replace part of Slide 8 examples.

### Mini-Case Blueprint (75-105 sec)
- `Task`: one clear objective.
- `Before`: old human-only workflow in 3-5 steps.
- `After`: agent-assisted workflow in 3-5 steps.
- `Delta`: time saved, quality change, human role shift.

### Candidate Case Topics
- Competitive research brief.
- Inbox triage + follow-up drafting.
- Bug triage and release-note prep.
- Sales outreach prep from public signals.

### Visual Format Ideas
- Option A: two-column comparison (`Before` vs `With Agent`).
- Option B: horizontal swimlane (`Human`, `Agent`, `Review`).
- Option C: loop diagram overlay with one concrete run.

### Example Structure (Template)
- `Goal`: “Prepare a competitor snapshot for Monday meeting.”
- `Before`: collect links, read, summarize, format deck.
- `After`: agent gathers sources, drafts synthesis, flags uncertainty, human final pass.
- `Result`: “From ~2.5h to ~35m; human effort moved to judgment.”

### Guardrails To Mention
- Human approves outbound actions.
- Claims must include source links.
- Anything high-impact gets a second review.

### Risks
- Sounds like a product demo: keep it narrative, not tool-specific.
- Sounds too magical: include one failure mode and correction.
