# Alt Outline C — "The Trust Arc"

**Arc:** Provocation → Delegation story → What trust requires → What it means
**Thesis:** Frame the ENTIRE talk around one question: "When do you hand over the wheel?"
**Tone:** Socratic — each slide sharpens one central question.

---

## Slide 1 — Title

**Clawd: When Intelligence Grew Hands**
Otto von Wachter

---

## Slide 2 — The provocation

"Would you let software send an email on your behalf?
Draft a contract?
Hire a contractor?
Make a medical appointment?
Invest your money?"

Speaker note: Start with a spectrum. Audience mentally places themselves on it. Everyone's line is in a different place. That's the talk.

---

## Slide 3 — "Something already changed."

For decades, AI was a brain in a jar. It could think. It could speak. It couldn't do.

Then — not with a press conference, but with a software update — it got hands.

Speaker note: Brief. Set up the "how" with minimal words.

---

## Slide 4 — The agent loop (how it works)

Goal → Context → Plan → Act → Observe → Evaluate → Repeat

"This is the architecture of doing. It's how humans work. Now machines work this way too."

Speaker note: Show the mechanism early so everything after is grounded.

---

## Slide 5 — Mini-case: Hiring a freelancer

**The task:** Find and vet a freelance designer for a one-week project.

**Before (~4 hours spread over 2 days):**
- Write job description
- Post on 2-3 platforms
- Review 15+ portfolios
- Shortlist 3-4 candidates
- Send outreach messages
- Schedule intro calls

**With agent (~45 min + human decisions):**
- Agent drafts job description from project brief
- Searches platforms, filters by criteria, ranks portfolios
- Drafts shortlist with reasoning for each pick
- Prepares personalized outreach messages
- Human reviews shortlist, adjusts tone, approves sends

**The moment:** You realize the agent did the *work* — but you still made every *decision*.

Speaker note: This example is about delegation specifically — it models the talk's central question. Not "can it do this?" but "am I comfortable letting it?"

---

## Slide 6 — Where's your line?

Spectrum (left to right):

**Comfortable** → Draft emails, summarize docs, schedule meetings
**Uneasy** → Send emails, make purchases, post publicly
**Not yet** → Legal decisions, medical choices, financial commitments

"Everyone has a line. The interesting question is: what determines where it falls?"

Speaker note: Interactive moment. Audience reflects on their own threshold.

---

## Slide 7 — The trust equation

Trust = three things:
- **Scope** — what it's allowed to do
- **Visibility** — can I see what it did and why
- **Reversibility** — can I undo a mistake quickly

"This is the same framework you use for human delegation. Manager to employee. Parent to teenager. The variables are identical."

Speaker note: The human analogy is the key insight. Delegation isn't new — the delegate is.

---

## Slide 8 — The five things people confuse for each other

When people argue about "AGI," they mix up:
- Intelligence (reasoning)
- Capability (skills)
- Agency (acting in the world)
- Autonomy (acting without asking)
- Reliability (getting it right)

"A brain in a jar: high intelligence, zero agency.
A Roomba: high agency, mediocre intelligence.
The interesting — and uncomfortable — zone is where they overlap."

---

## Slide 9 — What "general" actually means

General ≠ omniscient. General = transferable.

The same system that found your freelancer could also:
- Research a legal question
- Prepare a financial summary
- Draft a project plan
- Debug your code

Not because it learned each task — because it reasons about goals and tools.

Speaker note: Tie directly back to the hiring example.

---

## Slide 10 — The dangers of hands

The real risks aren't sci-fi:
- **Confident wrong actions** — it doesn't hesitate
- **Permission creep** — small grants compound into large access
- **Quiet failures** — errors nobody notices until they matter
- **Accountability blur** — "Who did this?" has no clear answer

"These aren't future risks. They're happening now, in every team using agents."

---

## Slide 11 — The human shift

When the doing is delegated, humans are left holding:
- **Judgment** — is this good enough?
- **Taste** — is this what we actually want?
- **Values** — should we be doing this at all?
- **Responsibility** — it acted, but I'm accountable

"The bottleneck was effort. Now the bottleneck is meaning."

---

## Slide 12 — The questions behind the question

- What work is worth doing ourselves — even when we don't have to?
- What does craft mean when the craft is delegated?
- Where does pride of authorship live now?

Speaker note: This is the quiet, slow slide. Let it breathe.

---

## Slide 13 — Back to the spectrum

(Callback to Slide 2)

"Would you let software send an email on your behalf?"

"Most of you already have."

"The line isn't fixed. It moves. The question is whether you move it deliberately — or let it drift."

Speaker note: Full circle. The provocation from the opening resolves here.

---

## Slide 14 — Closing

"The future is not AI replacing humans.
It's humans learning to direct intelligence that acts.
The hands are here. The question is what we build with them."

---

**Why this ordering might work better:**
- Single, clear through-line: trust and delegation
- Opens with audience self-reflection (where's YOUR line?) — immediately engaging
- The "spectrum" device at start and end creates narrative closure
- Every slide answers one sub-question of the central question
- Mini-case (hiring) directly models delegation, not just efficiency
- The AGI discussion serves the trust narrative rather than being its own section
- 14 slides, tight Socratic structure — each slide earns the next
- Most conversational of the three — feels like thinking together, not lecturing
